Data inventory · compiled 6 September 2026

The datasets

Every data source behind the empirical evaluation of NIST’s LEV metric — what was used, at what version, for which result, and what each one cannot tell us. Plus the six further sources identified for the next phase.

A note on terminology: nothing here is training data. This is a statistics and measurement study — no model is fitted to these sources, so they are analysis inputs and reference data, not a training set.

A coverage gap worth stating. Of the 1,676 CISA KEV entries, 1,665 have EPSS data. The remaining 11 have no EPSS score at any date and therefore cannot receive a LEV value at all — LEV is structurally silent on them.

Part 2

What each source cannot tell us

The limitations matter more than the counts. Each is documented at the line of code that introduces it; the study records 44 threats to validity in total.

SourceLimitation
EPSSSampled monthly rather than daily — measured cost 3.9% of mean LEV, and unsigned, so it adds noise not bias. Spans three model retrainings; one tripled corpus scores overnight, and LEV compounds across that boundary as though it were evidence of exploitation.
CISA KEVThree problems. (i) Incomplete by design — CISA lists only what it confirms and prioritises for federal remediation. (ii) dateAdded is when CISA catalogued, not when exploitation happened. (iii) It is an input feature to EPSS, so it partly predicts the very score being tested against it; removing that leak moves over-prediction from 11.9x to 32.7x.
CVE List v5Vendor names are unnormalised CNA free text, and a CVE affecting many products is attributed to the first listed. Per-vendor counts are lower bounds.
Exploit-DB
Metasploit		Record exploit availability, not exploitation in the wild — a different and much larger population. Both are also EPSS input features, so they correlate with LEV by construction. Valid as covariates; not valid as a second ground truth.

Provenance

How this stays reproducible

Pinned end dateThe EPSS archive end is fixed in code at 2026-08-01. Without it the loader defaults to today, and re-running weeks later silently ingests newer snapshots.
Committed sourcesExploit-DB and Metasploit are served upstream from moving branches, so both snapshots are committed to the repository rather than re-fetched.
Hashes recordedSHA-256 for every fixed-file source, with release tags, retrieval dates and record counts, in DATA_SNAPSHOT.md.
One commandmake fetch pulls the data once; make reproduce rebuilds every table and figure offline, in order.

Two licences remain unresolved. VulnCheck's attribution notice is silent on peer-reviewed publication of derived data and on archiving raw data for reproducibility, and the EPSS history repository declares no licence at all. Both need settling in writing before submission.
