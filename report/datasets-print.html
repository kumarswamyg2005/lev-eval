Phase 2 datasets

Phase 1’s blocker is that CISA KEV is our only exploitation ground truth, and it is incomplete. These are the sources that fix that — checked live for availability, cost, schema and licence rather than recalled.

Companion to LEV-calibration-Phase1.pdf  ·  machine-readable version in DATASETS.md

The dataset that unblocks the paper is free.

VulnCheck KEV Community needs a free account, not a licence. This was previously assumed to be a commercial purchase; it is not. There is no budget item in Phase 2.

Why this source and not simply a bigger one. Its schema is close to purpose-built for the estimator we need:

cve[], cwes[], vendorProject, product,
date_added                                 <- VulnCheck's own first-report date
cisa_date_added                            <- CISA's, inside the same record
vulncheck_reported_exploitation[]          <- {url, date_added} per reporting source
vulncheck_xdb[]                            <- {xdb_id, exploit_type, date_added}
reported_exploited_by_vulncheck_canaries   <- honeypot-observed (boolean)

cisa_date_added sitting inside every VulnCheck record means the CISA subset is identifiable without a join — the overlap that capture–recapture consumes comes free and unambiguous, with no fuzzy matching to defend in review. vulncheck_reported_exploitation[] goes further, naming each independent reporting source with its own date, so the estimator extends to many sources without acquiring them separately. reported_exploited_by_vulncheck_canaries is honeypot-observed exploitation — sensor evidence rather than curated advisories, so it is genuinely independent of the process that builds CISA KEV.

2. CISA KEV daily archive — fixes an existing threat, costs nothing

We currently hold one snapshot of KEV and trust its dateAdded field. Daily archives let us verify that field is never backdated or revised — README threat 17, presently stated but unverified. It also makes the as-of-date KEV rates in dn_sweep.py and plateau.py genuinely point-in-time rather than reconstructed from a single file.

Sourcegithub.com/cisagov/kev-data (official) or github.com/hrbrmstr/cisa-known-exploited-vulns (MIT, daily ISO-stamped snapshots)

4. GreyNoise — Community Research Program

Observed scanning and exploitation attempts from sensors. Its independence from KEV is the strongest of any source here: it observes traffic rather than curating advisories, so its blind spots are uncorrelated with CISA’s — which is exactly the assumption capture–recapture requires.

CostFree for non-commercial research — application required and reviewed, so apply early

Consequence

What this changes about the paper

With VulnCheck KEV alone, three things become possible for the first time.

  1. Estimate exploitation that neither catalog recorded, via stratified capture–recapture — replacing the vendor-ceiling bound whose weakness is already flagged as a limitation.
  2. Test whether the over-confidence shrinks against a broader ground truth. VulnCheck is ~3.6× larger, so if the 17.2× gap is mostly catalog blindness it should fall sharply; if it barely moves, that is evidence the residual is LEV error. Either result is publishable, which is what makes this the right next step rather than a gamble.
  3. Use vulncheck_reported_exploitation[] to stratify by reporting source and test whether the visibility bias found in CISA KEV — 32.5% of Microsoft CVEs against 2.3% of XWiki’s, at identical LEV — reproduces in an independent catalog.

Do not drop CISA KEV. The comparison between the two catalogs is the measurement, so both are required. VulnCheck replaces nothing.

Open

What could not be verified, and matters before submission

Licensing is unresolved on the two most important sources. VulnCheck publishes no formal redistribution terms — they request prominent attribution, but nothing states whether derived data may be published alongside a paper or archived for reproducibility. The EPSS historical repository declares no licence at all. One email to each settles both, and it should happen before submission rather than after review.
